How Indian Relay works

A team usually includes one rider, three horses, two holders, and a mugger, or catcher.

  1. 01 The lap

    The rider races one lap bareback and returns to the exchange area.

  2. 02 The exchange

    The rider leaps off and mounts the next horse.

  3. 03 The next horse

    The process repeats until all three horses have completed their laps.

The fastest clean team wins, subject to official rules. In PIRL’s words, Fort Hall is “a birthplace of modern competitive relay.” Horse culture among Native nations goes back centuries; the modern competitive sport developed in the 20th century.

What PIRL is building

A startup nonprofit league formed to organize and elevate Indian Relay Racing, and in its own words, “an Indigenous-led sports property that can grow with integrity.”

  • Standardized Platform

    Planned: consistent rules across events.

  • Safety, Welfare & Integrity

    Planned: horse welfare and safety standards.

  • Premier Events

    Planned: live events across North America.

  • Professional Media

    Planned: streaming and broadcast coverage.

  • Bigger Purses

    Planned: larger sponsorship and purse opportunities.

  • Championship Pathway

    Planned: a transparent points and championship pathway that honors the best teams in the sport.
